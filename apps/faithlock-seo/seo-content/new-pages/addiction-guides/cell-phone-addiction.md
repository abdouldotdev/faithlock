## What the term does and does not mean

“Cell phone addiction” is useful search language, not a diagnosis this page can make. The DSM-5 and ICD-11 do not provide a formal diagnosis with that name. That does not make the behavior harmless. A pattern is worth addressing when you keep opening apps you did not intend to open, cannot keep a boundary you chose, or continue despite clear effects on sleep, work, or relationships.

Do not use a screen-time number as a diagnosis. A person may need a phone for work or caregiving; another person may experience serious consequences with less total time. Look at control, purpose, and consequences instead.

## What to do on iPhone when willpower is not enough

### 1. Add a boundary at the point of use

Use iPhone settings to create a specific boundary for the apps or times that cause the most trouble. Turn off nonessential notifications and move high-trigger apps away from the Home Screen. The aim is to add a pause between impulse and access, not to claim that a setting guarantees a particular outcome.

Apple’s official support page explains Screen Time controls in the context of managing a child’s iPhone or iPad: [Apple Support 105121](https://support.apple.com/en-us/105121). Do not confuse that family-management documentation with a diagnosis or with professional care.

### 2. Add accountability you choose

Tell one trusted person the boundary you are trying to keep and when you will review it. Share a simple weekly summary such as which boundary you set and whether you kept it. This is voluntary accountability, not monitoring, surveillance, or a promise that another person can control your device.

### 3. Address the reason you reach for the phone

Notice whether the repeated check follows boredom, anxiety, loneliness, avoidance, or a work demand. Replace the automatic check with one concrete alternative: stand up, take a short walk, send a planned message, pray, or return to the task you named. If the phone is being used to manage persistent distress, address that distress rather than treating the device as the whole problem.

### 4. Seek professional help when impairment persists

Consider qualified professional support if you cannot reduce the pattern despite reasonable boundaries, or if phone use is causing ongoing distress or interfering with sleep, work, relationships, or safety. A professional can assess the broader situation without relying on the informal label “cell phone addiction.” FaithLock is not a medical treatment and this page does not promise a clinical result.
