## How to choose

Choose Apple Screen Time for the smallest setup: Apple documents App Limits and Downtime in its iPhone user guide. Choose Opal or Jomo when you want a dedicated session workflow and their documented focus protections fit your use case. Choose Freedom when you need app and website blocking across the devices it supports. Choose one sec when a pause before opening an app is more useful than a hard block. Choose ScreenZen when its delay, limits, schedules, and lock mode match your needs without a required subscription claim from its official site. Choose FaithLock when the desired friction is a Scripture pause rather than a generic timer.

No option here is a universal “best” or a guarantee against bypass. Before relying on a third-party blocker, review its current permissions and the relevant iOS settings. A blocker’s documented feature is not evidence of installs, purchases, payers, revenue, or a measurable behavior change.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best app blocker for iPhone?**

There is no best universal choice. Start with Apple Screen Time if built-in limits meet your needs. Otherwise choose the documented mechanism that fits: session protection, cross-device blocking, an intervention, delay-and-limit controls, or FaithLock’s Scripture pause.

**Can an iPhone blocker guarantee that I cannot bypass it?**

No guarantee is made here. Apple’s own documentation describes settings that can be changed, and Freedom explicitly documents an iOS Screen Time-permission limitation for Locked Mode. Treat anti-bypass language as a feature description, not proof that every workaround is closed.

**Is FaithLock a parental-control app?**

This page does not make that claim. The official FaithLock sources used here describe selected-app blocking and a Scripture/Bible-question step before access. They do not establish a parental-control guarantee.

**Does this comparison prove which app gets better results?**
