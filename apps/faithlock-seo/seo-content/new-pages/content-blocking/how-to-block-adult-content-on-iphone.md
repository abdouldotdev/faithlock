## Quick answer

Start with Apple’s own controls. Apple’s current guide for a child’s iPhone or iPad says to open **Settings**, tap **Family**, choose the child’s name, tap **Content & Privacy Restrictions**, then use **Content Restrictions** to choose age-based settings for supported content. Read [Apple’s current instructions](https://support.apple.com/en-us/105121) before changing the path, because the labels and available controls can vary by iOS version, account, country, and the person managing the device.

That is the documented Apple route. It is not a promise that every website, app, message, or account will be filtered, and Apple’s page is specifically written for parental controls on a child’s device.

## Apple’s method, step by step

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **Family**, then select the child’s name. Apple’s guide assumes Family Sharing and a child account.
3. Tap **Content & Privacy Restrictions**. Enter the Screen Time passcode if asked.
4. Open **Content Restrictions**.
5. Choose the relevant content type and an age-based setting, or **Don’t Allow**, **Unrated**, or **Allow All** where Apple provides those choices.
6. Review the result on the device and keep the Screen Time passcode with the parent or guardian who manages the restrictions.

Apple also documents controls for apps and websites, communication, privacy, and other settings on the same support page. Use the setting that matches the actual device and iOS version rather than copying an older menu path.

## What this does and does not answer

Apple documents controls for managing what a child can access. The support page does not establish a universal adult-content filter for every browser, app, message, or account. It also does not guarantee that a person managing their own iPhone cannot change the settings.

If you are setting up your own device, treat the Apple instructions as a starting point and verify the available controls directly in Settings. If your need is parental control, keep the account, Family Sharing, and passcode responsibilities with the parent or guardian rather than presenting a self-managed setting as a guarantee.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I block adult content on an iPhone?

For a child’s device, begin with Apple’s [Content & Privacy Restrictions guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/105121), then choose the appropriate settings under Content Restrictions. Apple’s page is for parental controls, so verify the exact options shown on your iPhone before relying on them.

### Does FaithLock block adult websites?

No. The cited FaithLock sources describe selected-app blocking with a Bible verse and prayer pause. They do not support a claim that FaithLock filters adult websites or messages.

### Does FaithLock replace Apple Screen Time parental controls?

No. FaithLock is not presented as a parental-control system. Use Apple’s controls for the parental-control task and consider FaithLock only for its separate app-blocking and prayer-pause use case.

### Can FaithLock prevent every workaround?

Do not assume that. The verified sources describe the app’s selected-app and Scripture-pause flow, not an anti-bypass guarantee. No guarantee is made here.

### What should I do if the Apple menu looks different?

Follow the current Apple Support page and the labels on your device. Apple’s instructions can change with iOS, Family Sharing, account type, region, and the role of the person managing the device.
