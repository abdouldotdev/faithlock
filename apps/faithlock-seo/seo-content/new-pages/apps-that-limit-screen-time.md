## What apps limit screen time on iPhone?

Apple Screen Time is the built-in starting point for setting app and device-use boundaries on iPhone. FaithLock is a separate iPhone and iPad app that its official sources describe as blocking selected apps and presenting Bible verses and prayer before access. Choose Screen Time for Apple's native controls; consider FaithLock only if a Scripture pause fits your intention. Neither is presented here as an unchangeable limit, monitoring service, parental-control system, anti-circumvention tool, or guarantee of a behavior result.

## Apple Screen Time: the native option

Apple documents Screen Time and parental controls as built-in ways to manage apps, websites, content, and device-use boundaries. The exact controls and menu names depend on your iOS version and setup, so confirm the current Apple instructions on your device.

For a personal app limit, open Settings and review Screen Time, App Limits, and the current app or website controls available to you. For a child’s device, Apple’s current instructions use Family Sharing, a child Apple Account, and a Screen Time passcode managed by a parent or guardian. These are different setups and should not be treated as interchangeable.

Screen Time is the clearest first choice when you want Apple’s own device-level controls. This page does not claim that a limit cannot be changed, that every app or website is covered in the same way, or that a setting guarantees a person will stop using an app.

## How to choose a screen-time boundary

1. Name the apps, websites, or time period that you want to review.
2. Check the current Screen Time options on your iPhone and decide whether the native controls match that boundary.
3. If you want a Scripture pause around selected apps, verify that FaithLock’s documented mechanism matches your intended use.
4. Keep emergency and essential access needs in mind, and do not describe either tool as a substitute for personal judgment or family care.
5. Reassess the boundary after using it. A setting or pause can create friction; it cannot promise what a person will choose next.
